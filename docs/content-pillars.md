# Content pillars and test strategy

All six series live on the same channel. These names are working labels; prioritize individual hooks over series labels in titles.

| Series | Viewer promise | Design focus | Effort hypothesis | First pilot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beat Arcade | Watch a game-like mechanism hit the beat | Bounce, clear, unlock, score | Low–medium | 1000 Bricks, 1 Ball |
| Beat Factory | Watch machines play a song | Cyclic machines, interlocking patterns, capacity | Medium | The Beat Factory |
| Rhythm Dungeon | Watch a fantasy encounter unfold musically | Attack tells, phases, magic, victory | Medium–high | Later |
| Controlled Chaos | Watch an ordered chain escalate | Cause-and-effect, sequence, satisfying resolution | Medium | Later |
| Rhythm Universe | Watch a tiny world come alive | Traffic, weather, worlds, coordination | Medium–high | Later |
| Beat Wars | Pick a side and see who wins | Competition, contrasting motifs, readable stakes | Medium | Red vs Blue: Beat Race |

## Experiment sequence
1. Make and approve The Beat Factory (satisfaction). Do not create the next pilots until its core production approach works.
2. Make Beat Race (competition) and 1000 Bricks (progression), each with a distinct soundtrack, scene and mechanic.
3. Publish in a comparable cadence (proposal: one per day per platform, subject to owner approval and existing queue). Maintain the same creative quality threshold; log topic, hook, runtime, music style, upload timing and production time.
4. If feasible, test three distinct episodes per pillar (18 total, including pilots) over roughly three weeks. This is an exploratory sample, not statistically conclusive. Avoid forcing three episodes of an obviously failing format before fixing it.
5. Review each upload after 48 hours and again at seven days, compare within each platform and similar runtime. For YouTube Shorts log stayed-to-watch/viewed-vs-swiped-away (as available in Studio), average view duration and percentage viewed, engaged views, shares, subscribers gained, and production effort. Use available retention curves to diagnose hook, build and payoff; average percentage viewed above 100% is a replay proxy, not an exact replay count. Record missing metrics as unavailable. Do not assume one viral outlier proves a series.
6. Select promising one or two pillars on sustained retention, shares/subscriber conversion and effort; iterate weak hooks and payoffs. Only then extract reusable engines, and only after the format works consider standalone 3–10 minute episodes with multiple genuinely distinct musical and visual sections.

No view, subscriber or monetization target is guaranteed. The test plan is a proposal, not analytics from the channel.
