# Creative direction — Square Wave Lab

## Identity
- Brand: Square Wave Lab / @squarewavelab.
- Tagline: Where Everything Becomes Music.
- Niche: music-led audiovisual entertainment; not science education.
- Audience: global, sound-on viewers; English minimal text, no narration by default.
- Format: original code-generated 3D pixel-art worlds, musical mechanics, satisfying escalation, clear payoff. Short-form first (roughly 30–45 seconds), vertical 9:16. Long-form later, designed as a musical journey rather than concatenated loops.
- Sound: hard techno as the anchor; acid/industrial techno variations first, EDM/DnB only as deliberate experiments. Preserve the approved sound reference and phone-speaker mix.
- Formula: **A playable world + a musical rule + an unexpected payoff.**

## The audience contract
Within two seconds the viewer should understand what is happening and have a reason to stay. At least one immediately legible musical action should occur in the opening frames. Use visible stakes (race lead, remaining targets, machine overload, unlocks, chain completion), not an educational lecture or vague promise. The payoff should change the world, not merely increase screen shake or particle density. Make the video enjoyable with sound on and intelligible muted; do not treat these as substitutes for synchronization.

Every musically meaningful sound has an identifiable on-screen cause; every salient rhythmic action has an audible representation. Incidental motion need not create a separate note. Sustained tones map to visible sustained states, while short hits map to contact, activation or release. Do not add unrelated effects to hide missing mappings. Compose an actual song, with variation, harmonic agreement and a memorable hook, not a MIDI demo of random physics.

## Visual grammar
Retain the existing offscreen Three.js pixel pipeline (360×640 logical scene, Endesga 32 palette, ordered dithering, 3× output to 1080×1920, crisp HUD), fonts and safe areas unless a pilot proves a justified change. Fill the frame with readable 3D scenes. Distinct silhouettes and colors encode instruments and teams. The camera must preserve the causal moment; cut only if the viewer can still read the action. Prefer diegetic animation (pistons, gates, impacts, sparks caused by events) over decorative flashes. Avoid strobing and overwhelming effects. Keep captions short; no code card, formula explanation, or lesson recap for entertainment. Preserve the existing science aesthetic and rules on legacy productions.

## Narrative forms
- Satisfaction: a system becomes increasingly coordinated.
- Competition: a fair-looking race or challenge develops, then resolves.
- Progression: a simple mechanism unlocks new capabilities and pays off.
- Surprise: a visible setup leads to an unexpected but understandable result.

## Musical design
Choose tempo, meter, harmonic palette, motifs, instrument roles, section plan and visual causality together before simulation development. Follow the existing CLAUDE.md requirements: vary at least three musical characteristics versus each of the last three productions, clear call-and-answer, meaningful changes at least every four bars in hook and eight bars in arrangement, three audible parts in main sections, forward kick/bass, audible melody, and no unmotivated competing rhythmic patterns. Retain approved mastering and analysis tools. A measured mix is not proof of musical quality; owner listening is required.

## What changes, what doesn't
Real physics becomes optional: authored trajectories, seeded game rules, quantized contacts and musical scheduling are allowed. Never represent fictional or staged mechanics as an authentic real-world result. Preserve deterministic rendering, shared clock, original audio, sync budget, art direction and release packaging. Legacy content remains intact. Use entertainment-led titles (not science questions) with honest hooks. No unrelated trend audio, copyrighted melodies without rights, false gameplay claims, or mass-produced seed-only variants.
