**What's true in this video:** real time, no warp. An ideal ball (no air drag) dropped from 19.6 m, so the fall takes exactly 2 s; bounciness (coefficient of restitution) e = 1/2, so every bounce leaves at half the impact speed and lasts half as long: 2 s, 1 s, 0.5 s... The flights add up to 4 s, so infinitely many bounces finish at t = 10 s. A real ball stops after a few dozen bounces (said on screen). The view zooms in as the bounces shrink; the ruler shows the scale. The claw is a prop for the loop.

**Physics:** the build. Every impact is a snare hit plus a note that climbs the chord one step per bounce, and because each flight halves, the hits land on the bar, half bar, quarter, 8th, 16th, 32nd: an accelerating roll that ends exactly on the downbeat where the music drops. **Groove, not physics:** the "dem" kick tuned to each chord (F, Db, Ab, Eb), the bass on the same roots, the drop's lead hook, snare, clap, hats, pad, the falling and lifting glides.

**Music:** chiptune house, 120 BPM, F minor (Fm, Db, Ab, Eb). Every band measures 120 BPM; kick body measured on each chord root (70, 51, 38, 43 Hz). Loop check: PASS. Mix and loudness measured, not yet listened to by the channel owner.
