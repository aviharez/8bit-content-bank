**What's true in this video:** the rim is a driven, damped oscillator. Its steady-state motion, relative to the same push held still, is A = 1 / |1 - r^2 + i r / Q| with r = f / f0, and it follows the drive with the envelope time constant 2Q / (2 pi f0) = 0.36 s. This glass is given f0 = 880 Hz (A5) and Q = 1,000 (an assumed value, said in the YouTube description; real crystal glasses are in that range but each one differs). At its own note the rim moves 1,000x, one semitone away 9x: 109x less. The breaking point is set where the rim is on the downbeat of the drop (we chose it; the video does not claim a real breaking strain). Rim motion is exaggerated on screen, and the rim's two extreme shapes (the n = 2 oval mode) alternate every frame because 880 Hz is far faster than 30 fps. The bench rings are one wavelength apart (343 m/s / f). The shards fly with real ballistics (gravity, one bounce, restitution 0.3) at 1/8 speed, then rewind for the loop.

**Picture (3D, every effect shows the physics):** a speaker and a wine glass on a lab bench. The speaker cone pushes on every note and its halo lights while it plays; the rings on the bench show the wavelength of each note; the glass glows and its rim swings as the amplitude climbs (HUD: the note, its frequency, the rim motion as a number and a log meter to the breaking point). The break is the one moment with a white flash and a camera hit. The shards fall in slow motion, the camera circles them, then everything rewinds into the first frame.

**Physics in the sound:** the lead is the speaker: the exact notes that drive the simulated glass, and its volume rises 4x while it holds A5. The glass's own voice is a held A5 whose volume follows the simulated rim amplitude: it swells every time the melody lands on A5 and stops dead on the drop. Every shard landing is a high chord tone (glockenspiel), snapped to the nearest 16th (at most 50 ms). **Groove, not physics:** the "dem" kick four on the floor tuned to each chord root (A, F, C, G), clap, 16th and offbeat hats, reese bass on the roots with an offbeat octave roll, pad, riser, snare roll, impact, supersaw offbeat stabs and the hook on a supersaw in the drop, a rewind sweep back into the loop.

**Music:** EDM / techno, 150 BPM, A minor (Am, F, C, G). Dense from 0 s: loudness per 4 s stays within 1.2 dB end to end. The drop (the break) lands at 25.6 s. Onsets sit on the 150 BPM 16th grid (low 58%, mid 73%, hats 79% within 15 ms of a 16th, against 30% by chance). Key estimate: A minor. Dem body measured on F1 (43 Hz) in F bars. Loop check: PASS.
