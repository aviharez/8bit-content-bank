**What's true in this video:** every heart rate is the scaling law for resting mammals, heart rate = 241 × M^-1/4 beats a minute, M in kg (Stahl 1967). Blue whale 100 t: 13.6. Elephant 5 t: 28.7. Human 70 kg: 83. Cat 4 kg: 170. Rabbit 2 kg: 203. Mouse 20 g: 641. Etruscan shrew 1.8 g (the smallest mammal by mass): 1,170, which is 19.5 beats a second. These are predictions of the law, not measurements of one animal (measured resting rates scatter around the line). Real time, no warp: each animal gets 2 bars (the shrew 8), and its heartbeats fall at the true intervals.

**Physics:** every predicted heartbeat is a monitor beep, snapped to the nearest 16th note (at most 42 ms; said on screen), playing the next note of the current chord, higher for smaller animals. The shrew's 19.5 Hz is faster than the 16th grid (12 a second), so it is not beeps: it is a held D#4 (19.5 × 16 = 312 Hz, 4 octaves up) whose volume pulses at exactly 19.5 Hz. That note is the drop. **Groove, not physics:** the "dem" kick tuned to each chord root (D#, B, F#, C#) in a drum and bass two-step, reese bass on the same roots, snare, ghost snares, hats, pad, riser, snare roll, supersaw hook in the drop.

**Picture effects, all locked to the same timeline:** the frame shakes on every kick (hardest on the drop and on the whale's and elephant's heartbeats), trembles one pixel every frame through the shrew's drop, a white flash opens the drop, the background and HUD flash on drop kicks, the heart grows and throws a ring on every beat, glitches (red/cyan split) on drop kicks, and sparks fly off it on every 16th.

**Music:** drum and bass, 180 BPM, D# minor (D#m, B, F#, C#). The drop lands at 16 s, halfway. Onsets sit on the 180 BPM 16th grid (low 49%, hats 79% within 15 ms of a 16th, against 36% by chance). Fine chroma: D#, C#, F#, A#, B (D# minor). The dem body measures 38 Hz (D#1) in the intro; in the drop the reese on the same roots carries the low band. Loop check: PASS.
