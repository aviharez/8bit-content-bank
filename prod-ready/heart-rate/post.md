# How Fast Can a Heart Beat: ready to post

Series: How Life Works · Channel: Square Wave Lab (`@squarewavelab`)

## As a stranger sees it

- **After 3 seconds:** a blue whale under a big beating heart in a lab, the question "How fast can a heart beat?", and a red pulse running round a tube: each red pulse is one heartbeat.
- **By the end:** the smaller the body, the faster the heart: a whale about once every 4 seconds, you about 80 a minute, a cat twice that, a hamster 7 a second, and the tiniest mammal so fast its beats turn into one continuous note; one simple rule fits all of them.

**What's true in this video:** every heart rate is the scaling law for resting mammals, 241 × M^-1/4 beats a minute, M in kg (Stahl 1967). Blue whale 100 t: 13.6. Elephant 5 t: 28.7. Human 70 kg: 83. Cat 4 kg: 170. Rabbit 2 kg: 203. Hamster 100 g: 429. Etruscan shrew 1.8 g (the smallest mammal by mass): 1,170, which is 19.5 beats a second. These are the law's predictions, not measurements of one animal. Real time, no warp: each animal gets 2 bars (the shrew 8), and its heartbeats fall at the true intervals; each beep is nudged onto the nearest 16th note, by 50 ms at most (said on screen in plain words). The hamster replaced the mouse of v1: at 150 BPM the 16th grid runs 10 a second, and a mouse (10.7 a second) would not fit it; the hamster (7.1) does, so the shrew stays the only heart that breaks the grid.

**Picture (3D, every effect shows the physics):** a voxel heart over the animal on a turntable in a lab. Every heartbeat contracts the heart, lights the room and pushes one red pulse round the vessel loop in 2 s, so the gap between pulses is the gap between beats (one for the whale, a chain for the hamster, no gaps left for the shrew). The rig shrinks with each animal while the room keeps its size. The only camera shake is on the whale's and the elephant's beats. Outro: all seven on one straight line, then back to the whale for the loop. HUD: the animal and its mass, beats a minute.

**Every thing on screen is an instrument (the reference's rule):** each heartbeat is the heart monitor (a bleep on the next chord note, higher for smaller animals) plus a chord stab; the shrew's 19.5 a second merges them into one held chord whose volume pulses at exactly 19.5 Hz, topped by D#4 (19.5 × 16 = 312 Hz): the drop, with a dark riff. **Groove:** hard techno, the tuned "dem" four on the floor with rumble, clap on 2 and 4, 16th hats, offbeat open hat, distorted reese wall in legato 16ths, pad, riser, snare roll into the drop.

**Music:** hard techno, 150 BPM, D# minor (D#m, B, G#m, A#), the house sound of `docs/music-reference.md`. Dense and level: loudness per 4 s within 0.7 dB end to end. All bands measure the same tempo (75 = half of 150). Loop check: PASS. Mix and loudness measured, not yet listened to by the channel owner.
