**What's true in this video:** real time, no warp. Sound spreads at 343 m/s; one ring is drawn per 8th note (the fronts are continuous; the rings sample them). A listener straight ahead hears the engine shifted by 1 / (1 - M). The speed is chosen so that factor lands on harmonics (2x at Mach 0.5, 3x at 0.667, 4x at 0.75, 6x, 8x, 12x ... 128x), each new step starting on the grid after a quarter-beat throttle ramp; it goes to infinity at Mach 1 (16 s, the drop). Supersonic, from Mach 1 to 2: nothing is heard ahead, the fronts trail in a cone of half-angle asin(1/M) (90 to 30 degrees, shown live). The towers stand beside the path, placed (solved numerically) so the boom, the first arrival of any front made since Mach 1, reaches each one exactly on a kick. Outro: back down through Mach 1 and the harmonics to Mach 0.5, the opening state. The jet is drawn far larger than to scale (said on screen); 1 scene unit = 100 m.

**Picture (3D, every effect shows the physics):** a chase camera above a jet over fields. The rings (white when new, fading to blue) pile up ahead as it speeds up; a vapour collar forms around Mach 1; the frame whites out once at Mach 1 with the only camera hit; then a translucent Mach cone and its two edge lines trail behind, and each tower's beacon flashes, with a shock ring on the ground, when the cone sweeps over it. The afterburner grows with speed. HUD: Mach number and m/s, the note and factor heard ahead (or the cone angle when supersonic).

**Physics in the sound:** the engine as heard straight ahead (engine tone D1 times the Doppler factor) steps up the harmonics 2x, 3x, 4x, 6x, 8x ... (D and A only), faster and faster: that climb is the riser. Silent while supersonic. Every kick in the drop is a tower's boom, with an extra boom layer; Mach 1 gets the impact and crash. In the outro the engine voice returns from the top and steps back down to 2x. **Groove, not physics:** the tuned "dem" kick in a drum and bass two-step from the first frame, snare, ghosts and fills, 16th hats, rolling reese on the chord roots (D, Bb, F, C), pad, 16th arp, a lead hook in the drop, snare roll into Mach 1.

**Music:** drum and bass, 180 BPM, D minor (Dm, Bb, F, C; bars 8-11 hold Dm under the climb). Dense from 0 s: loudness per 4 s stays within 1.6 dB end to end. Onsets on the 16th grid: low 54%, mid 69%, hats 81% within 15 ms (36% by chance). Fine chroma: D, F, C, A#, A (D minor). Loop check: PASS.
